Next: SBC & Convention Watch.

Ohio Baptists send 33 missionaries. The State Convention of Baptists in Ohio held its Annual Celebration Nov. 9–10 at First Baptist Church Kettering's Sugarcreek campus near Dayton. On the first evening, an IMB Sending Celebration affirmed 33 missionaries from 10 states, two of them from Ohio, bound for places including Colombia, Albania, and the Pacific Islands. IMB President Paul Chitwood thanked Ohio Baptists for their record Cooperative Program giving. Asked what she would tell churches, one departing missionary said simply, "Jesus is enough." The next Sending Celebration is Jan. 27 in Marietta, Georgia.
Most SBC churches confident, but the bench is thin. A Lifeway Research survey of 1,044 Southern Baptist congregations, part of the Faith Communities Today study, found 86% say their future is at least OK and 27% say they are thriving. Finances are steady: 68% rate them good or excellent, and 72% ended their last fiscal year in the black. Online worship now reaches 62% of churches, up from 24% in 2019. The warning signs are in the leadership and the pews: 56% have a single pastor with no associate, the average pastor is 56, 42% of senior pastors hold another paid job, 36% have no youth activities, and 44% have none for young adults. Elders should ask who will shepherd their church in fifteen years, and who is being trained now.
Week of Prayer for International Missions is two weeks out. Southern Baptists observe the Week of Prayer Nov. 29–Dec. 6. Pathway editor Benjamin Hawkins offers nine habits for keeping the nations before a church all year, from praying for a named missionary to reading missionary biographies and noticing the nations in your own town.
Announcements from the conventions and networks. North Carolina Baptists meet Nov. 16–17 in Greensboro under the theme "On Mission Together," with Trevin Wax preaching the convention sermon and a Sending Celebration for missionaries. Texas Baptists (BGCT) meet Nov. 15–17 in Waco. Harbor Network's digital town hall is Nov. 19, with an Alignment Intensive by Zoom on Dec. 15.

Next: Reformed & Confessional.

9Marks: the gathering is not optional for discipleship. In a Nov. 12 article, U.K. pastor Joshua George argues that the weekly gathering is essential to ongoing discipleship, not just to the start of the Christian life. He points to the Lord's Day pattern of Acts 20:7, the command of Hebrews 10:24–25, and the fact that the New Testament letters were written to gathered churches, where the Word is preached and the Supper is shared together. "We never graduate and move beyond the local church." Worth putting in front of members who have drifted to watching online.
9Marks: counsel with the Great Commission in view. Nick Gardner of Capitol Hill Baptist Church reminds pastors that Jesus gave the Great Commission to disciples who were afraid and doubting. He draws four lessons for counseling from Matthew 28:18–20: Christ's authority over every situation, disciple-making as the goal, Christ's teaching as the content, and Christ's presence as the hope. Jonathan Leeman's Nov. 6 piece makes a related point: conversion makes a person part of a family, not just a forgiven individual.
PCA: a national church in Paraguay. byFaith reports that elders from Potomac Presbytery and Paraguayan elders formally organized the Iglesia Presbiteriana Nacional de Paraguay in Asunción, meeting a goal set by a 2015 General Assembly overture: three organized churches and three teaching elders. The new denomination has 13 elders across five churches.

Next: Theology & Doctrine.

Preaching as defense of the faith. In byFaith, RUF minister Derek Rishmawy reviews Rob Edwards's Pulpit Apologetics (Westminster Seminary Press), which argues that proclamation and apologetics belong together and that every faithful sermon defends the message it announces. Rishmawy calls it "a careful, clear, and needed volume for preachers," though he wanted more worked examples. Useful for any elder who preaches to a room that includes doubters.
What songs should a church sing? In Christianity Today, a Texas worship leader argues that churches should not simply ban every song from Hillsong, Bethel, or Elevation, because singing a song is not an endorsement of the ministry behind it. He grants some limits and recommends CityAlight, the Gettys, and the hymns. Whatever your church's policy, the article is a good prompt for elders to state plainly why they choose the songs they do: for what the words teach, and for who is shaping the congregation's taste.

Next: Culture & Church-State.

After the preschool argument, a likely win for religious liberty. Writing in Christianity Today after the Nov. 3 argument in St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, political scientist Daniel Bennett counts "at least five—and likely six" justices poised to side with the Catholic parish, which was shut out of Colorado's universal pre-K program over its teaching on sex and marriage. Several justices questioned whether Employment Division v. Smith should still govern free-exercise cases; a narrower ruling could rest on Carson v. Makin. A decision is expected by June.
Making sense of the midterms. In The Pathway, Jeremiah Greever calls the Nov. 3 results a landslide for Democrats and names Missouri's failed Amendment 3 as the most troubling outcome. He urges lament, perspective, faithful civic action, and hope in God's sovereignty: "Christian, never forget it's the Gospel that changes hearts, not politics."
Terror attack on a church in Jordan. Three gunmen opened fire on a funeral of about 150 people at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak governorate, killing nine and wounding at least 34, the worst terrorist attack in Jordan in a decade. Police arrested three suspects and the government declared three days of mourning. The Bible Society of Jordan printed thousands of booklets of comfort for the grieving. "A church should be the safest place on earth," its general secretary said. Pray for Jordan's roughly 200,000 Christians.

Next: For the Elder's Desk.

The Lifeway numbers are encouraging and sobering at once. Most churches feel healthy, but many rest on one aging pastor, often working a second job, with little ministry to the young. Ohio's 33 missionaries remind us that the Lord still raises up workers, and that they come from churches that pray for them and train them. 9Marks points the same way: discipleship happens in the gathered church, Sunday by Sunday, not apart from it. So ask: who in our congregation could be the next elder, deacon, or missionary, and is anyone deliberately training them? And then: as the Week of Prayer for International Missions approaches, how will we lead our people to pray for the nations by name?
