SBC & Convention Watch

Annual meeting, entities, ERLC, resolutions, denominational politics.

Pastors are wary of AI reading Scripture for them — but they are using it anyway. A Barna study with Gloo, surveying 442 senior Protestant pastors in late 2025, finds 94% concerned about AI interpreting Scripture, far above practicing Christians (83%) and the general public (74%). Only 5% reject AI outright as a tool on hard passages; 60% want it to lay out the major interpretations without choosing, and 10% want it to name the right one. The number to sit with: 26% of practicing Christians say they trust AI to determine the correct interpretation. Your people may already be asking a chatbot what your sermon text means. The Pathway (BP)

ERLC’s Lenow calls for action after a court shields AI-generated child abuse images. The U.S. 7th Circuit affirmed dismissal of a possession charge against a Wisconsin man over AI-generated child sexual abuse images, relying on Stanley v. Georgia and Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition; distribution and production charges proceed. ERLC President Evan Lenow said the time is “now for action” to prohibit producing such material with AI. Expect this to become a legislative ask from Southern Baptists this fall, and a reason to revisit your church’s child-protection and technology policies. The Pathway (BP)

Reformed & Confessional

Reformed Baptist, Presbyterian (PCA/OPC), confessional churches and networks.

The PCA answers the women-deacons dissent: “Here the matter shall end.” After June’s Assembly rejected Overture 37 (Pacific Presbytery’s request to let sessions decide whether qualified women may be deacons), four teaching elders — Aaron Baker, Jeffrey Choi, Walter Henegar, and Eric Kapur — filed a dissent calling the committee’s grounds circular. A commission of Ligon Duncan, David Garner, Thomas Gibbs, Jonathan Master, and Guy Waters has now published the Assembly’s answer, making exegetical, historical, and constitutional arguments that the office is limited to men while sessions may appoint godly men and women to assist the deacons. Under the Book of Church Order, that closes the matter procedurally, though not necessarily in the presbyteries. Baptist churches with deacon debates of their own will find both documents worth reading side by side. byFaith WORLD

The OPC’s week of prayer and fasting begins today. As noted last week, the OPC General Assembly has called its congregations to pray and fast Sept. 13–19, asking the Lord of the harvest to raise up and equip men for ministry at home and abroad. OPC

Founders on the “Same, Lindsay” phenomenon. David Mitzenmacher’s Sept. 11 article follows his earlier piece on young women by examining the online sympathy for Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts mother whose trial for killing her three children ended in a mistrial. He argues that it reflects a “disordered empathy” and a view of children’s dependence as injury, and that Christian theology does better justice to both the goodness and the real burdens of motherhood. Also new: a Sword & the Trowel interview with Tou Thao, one of the officers convicted in the George Floyd case, on his conversion in prison. Articles and resources, not news. Theology & Doctrine

Major debates, books, controversies, council and committee statements.

The Global Methodist Church adopts Articles of Faith and elects its first full-time bishops. Closing Sept. 5 in Johannesburg, the GMC’s General Conference adopted a doctrinal statement covering God, Scripture, humanity, salvation, the church, sacraments, and the Christian life, and elected eight bishops to six-year terms beginning Nov. 1, including Carolyn Moore. Four years after leaving the United Methodist Church over sexuality and biblical authority, the GMC is now a confessionally defined denomination that ordains women to its highest office — a useful case study in how “traditionalist” is defined differently across communions. Culture & Church-State

Religious liberty, ethics, politics affecting the church, cultural engagement.

Illinois assisted suicide takes effect — with religious exemptions only by court order. As of Sept. 12, terminally ill adults in Illinois with less than six months to live may request lethal medication under the law Gov. JB Pritzker signed last December. Only plaintiffs who sued have relief so far: a restraining order covers four physicians, the Lutheran Care Center, and Diocese of Springfield hospitals (represented by Thomas More Society), and a federal judge separately barred enforcement against the Archdiocese of Chicago, the Carmelite Sisters for the Aged and Infirm, and the Little Sisters of the Poor. Pastors in any state should expect to counsel families facing this choice, and should know which local hospitals and hospices will and will not participate. The Pathway (BP) Chicago Catholic

Also Worth Noting

Notable controversy and media shake-ups — reported with discernment, not appetite.

ACNA’s Wood trial: first week ends, verdict far off. The ecclesiastical trial of Archbishop Steve Wood, presided over by Bishop Ryan Reed, ran Sept. 7–12 in Charleston, closed to the public, with Sept. 21–26 held in reserve. The charges remain violation of ordination vows, abuse of ecclesiastical power, and sexual immorality; Wood denies most allegations, and the court denied his motion to dismiss in June. A judgment may take up to 101 days after the trial concludes, so do not expect clarity before winter. VirtueOnline RNS

For the Elder’s Desk

A pastoral takeaway for your leadership team this week.

Put two numbers next to each other: 94% of pastors worry about AI interpreting Scripture, and 26% of practicing Christians already trust it to. The gap is not a technology problem; it is a teaching problem. People turn to whatever voice answers their questions quickly and confidently, and if the church has not taught them how to read the Bible — context, the analogy of faith, the humility to sit with a hard text — they will outsource that to the fastest voice available. The same is true when a family calls from a hospital in Illinois, or anywhere, with a question about ending a loved one’s suffering. So ask your board this week: do our members know how to open the Scriptures for themselves, and would they call an elder before they ask a search box? If you are not sure, consider a short class this fall on how to read the Bible, and make sure every elder knows how to answer the end-of-life question before it comes. “Always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect” (1 Pet. 3:15). Prepared people are taught people.
