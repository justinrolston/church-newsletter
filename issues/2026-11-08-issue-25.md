# State of the Church Today

*A weekly digest of Southern Baptist, Reformed, and broader theological news — curated for pastors and elders.*

**Issue 25 · Sunday, November 8, 2026**

> Tuesday's ballots went mostly against the pro-life cause: Missouri kept its abortion amendment, Nevada and Virginia wrote abortion into their constitutions, and Virginia also redefined marriage. Idaho was the exception, rejecting an abortion-rights measure by 12 points. A new Lifeway study finds that most churchgoers would fail a basic Bible quiz, and Missouri Baptists signed on to an SBC-wide goal of 450,000 baptisms by 2033. The Supreme Court heard a Colorado case that could decide whether religious preschools can keep their convictions and still receive public funds. In the UK, former Acts 29 chief executive Steve Timmis and his wife have been charged with rape. A week to grieve losses, teach the Bible plainly, and keep watch over the flock.

## ⛪ SBC & Convention Watch

*Annual meeting, entities, ERLC, resolutions, denominational politics.*

**Missouri Baptists sign on to 450,000 baptisms by 2033.** At their Oct. 26–27 annual meeting in St. Charles, 1,183 messengers from 494 churches stood to affirm the SBC-wide goal of 450,000 baptisms by 2033, the 2,000th anniversary of the Great Commission. Churches have reported 7,821 baptisms so far this year, and the convention expects 2026 to be its sixth straight year of growth. Executive Director Wes Fowler was blunt about where such an effort lives: "The convention can't really lead this initiative... Any type of baptism initiative must be led by the local church." Messengers also approved a sorrowful plan to wind down the insolvent Baptist Homes & Healthcare Ministries. The Missouri Baptist Children's Home will take on senior care and two campuses, and the other five will be sold. They adopted a $15.3 million Cooperative Program goal and passed resolutions backing Amendment 3 and opposing sports betting. — [The Pathway](https://mbcpathway.com/2026/11/06/missouri-baptist-messengers-embrace-2033-sbc-wide-baptism-initiative-approve-plan-of-transition-for-baptist-homes-mbch/) · [The Pathway](https://mbcpathway.com/2026/11/02/missouri-baptist-speak-for-amendment-3-against-sports-betting/)

**Most churchgoers fail a Bible literacy quiz.** Lifeway Research gave more than 2,000 Christian churchgoers dozens of basic questions about Scripture, and the average score was 51. Nearly two-thirds (65%) scored an F, and only 9% earned an A. Nearly everyone knew Moses received the Ten Commandments (94%), but only 45% could name Saul as Israel's first king, and only 11% could put Deborah, David and Goliath, Jonah, and Esther in order. Eighty percent could complete John 3:16; only 28% knew Micah 6:8. "This study confirms most Christian churchgoers haven't learned or don't remember many things in the Bible and how it is organized," said Scott McConnell. Read it beside last week's Lifeway findings on discipleship habits. — [The Pathway (Lifeway Research)](https://mbcpathway.com/2026/11/06/most-churchgoers-fail-bible-literacy-quiz/)

**A coming shortage of worship leaders.** A study by SBC Worship found that 70% of worship leaders nearing retirement have not identified a successor, and projects that Southern Baptists will need 20,000 new worship leaders by 2040. Christianity Today reports that churches and colleges are trying residencies and structured mentorship to raise up the next generation. Smaller congregations should plan now for who will lead the singing in ten years. — [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2026/11/worship-leader-shortage-churches-training-megachurch/)

**Texas Baptists survey churches on CP giving.** A Baptist General Convention of Texas task force studying Cooperative Program giving trends will email a survey to its churches in the coming days. Texas Baptists meet Nov. 15–17 in Waco. — [Baptist Standard](https://baptiststandard.com/news/texas/cooperative-program-task-force-to-distribute-church-giving-survey/)

**Announcements from Ohio and the networks.** The State Convention of Baptists in Ohio holds its Annual Celebration this week at First Baptist Kettering–Sugarcreek in Dayton, with an IMB Sending Ceremony the evening of Nov. 9 and the annual meeting Nov. 10. Florida, South Carolina, and Louisiana Baptists also meet this week, and North Carolina and Alabama follow Nov. 16–18. Send Network hosts a "Raising Up Replanters" webinar Nov. 12 for churches that want to develop pastors to revitalize dying congregations. Harbor Network's digital town hall is Nov. 19, with an Alignment Intensive by Zoom on Dec. 15. Liberty Church Network posted its November newsletter Nov. 5. — [SCBO](https://www.scbo.org/ac-home) · [Florida Baptist Convention](https://flbaptist.org/know-before-you-go-what-to-expect-at-the-2026-florida-baptist-state-convention-annual-meeting/) · [Send Network](https://www.sendnetwork.com/events/raising-up-replanters-webinar/) · [Harbor Network](https://harbornetwork.com/events-feed/alignment-dec-2026)

## 📖 Reformed & Confessional

*Reformed Baptist, Presbyterian (PCA/OPC), confessional churches and networks.*

**9Marks on telling godly grief from worldly grief.** In a Nov. 2 article from its Conversion journal, Texas pastor Sam Crites draws three marks of true repentance from 2 Corinthians 7:10: mourning over sin and not merely its consequences, a desire for reconciliation whatever it costs, and an earnest wish to please God rather than the world. He reminds pastors that the fruit of godly grief usually shows over time. Useful for elders weighing a restoration or a membership interview. This week's Missions Talk episode takes up how sending churches, receiving churches, and agencies can share the care of missionaries. — [9Marks](https://www.9marks.org/article/discerning-between-godly-and-worldly-grief/) · [9Marks](https://www.9marks.org/episode/on-a-church-centered-approach-to-member-care-an-agencys-perspective/)

## 🕊️ Theology & Doctrine

*Major debates, books, controversies, council and committee statements.*

**Psychology Today calls porn "not a public health crisis."** An October Psychology Today article by a London psychotherapist argued that pornography's critics induce shame, that "ethical porn" can "enhance people's well-being," and that the answer is "porn literacy," not recovery programs, some of which he says carry "hidden religiosity." ERLC President Evan Lenow pushed back in a Baptist Press report. The article is a clear sign that parts of the therapeutic world now treat Christian sexual ethics as the problem. Elders should expect members, and members' counselors, to hear this message, and should make sure the church's own care for those caught in porn is gospel-shaped rather than merely shame-shaped. — [Biblical Recorder (Baptist Press)](https://www.brnow.org/news/psychology-today-claims-porn-beneficial-draws-rebuke-of-erlcs-lenow/)

**"Watch for God's presence to fall."** Outgoing Missouri Baptist president Wesley Vance preached 2 Chronicles 7 to messengers and called pastors back to prayer and repentance: "Our problem is the pastor's prayer room. We have left the vacancy sign out hanging." A fitting word set beside the Bible literacy numbers above. — [The Pathway](https://mbcpathway.com/2026/11/03/vance-tells-mbc-to-watch-for-gods-presence-to-fall/)

## 🏛️ Culture & Church-State

*Religious liberty, ethics, politics affecting the church, cultural engagement.*

**Pro-life losses on Tuesday, with one exception.** Missouri voters rejected Amendment 3 by about 55% to 45%, leaving in place the abortion-rights amendment passed in 2024; the measure would also have barred gender transition procedures for minors. Nevada and Virginia added a right to abortion to their constitutions. Idaho went the other way: 56% rejected Proposition 1, which would have created a right to abortion before viability, and the state's ban stands. "This is what happens when Christians speak out and get engaged," said Blaine Conzatti of the Idaho Family Policy Center. In Virginia, voters also approved by about 57% an amendment replacing the state's marriage definition with a right for two adults to marry regardless of sex. Legal challenges to certification of the Virginia amendments are pending. Americans United for Life called the results "devastating" and urged earlier, clearer teaching about what ballot language actually does. — [Florida Phoenix (States Newsroom)](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/11/04/repub/idaho-voters-reject-abortion-ballot-measure-as-missouri-nevada-and-virginia-back-access/) · [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2026/11/midterms-abortion-prolife-trump-missouri-nevada-virginia-idaho/) · [Axios Richmond](https://www.axios.com/local/richmond/2026/11/04/virginia-election-results-constitutional-amendments)

**Supreme Court hears the Colorado preschool case.** On Nov. 3 the justices heard *St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy*, in which two Denver-area Catholic preschools were shut out of Colorado's universal pre-K program because they ask families to support church teaching on sex and marriage. Colorado says it applies a neutral rule to all schools; the preschools, represented by Becket, argue that *Carson v. Makin* forbids conditioning public benefits on giving up religious convictions. Justices Jackson, Sotomayor, and Kagan appeared firmly opposed. Justice Barrett asked how a rule known to push out dissenting believers differs from explicit exclusion, and Justice Gorsuch questioned why strict scrutiny should not apply. Any church running a preschool, school, or daycare should follow the ruling. — [WORLD](https://wng.org/roundups/mandate-for-inclusivity-or-religious-discrimination-1793828807)

**Vermont tuition lawsuit.** Alliance Defending Freedom has filed suit on behalf of a Vermont family barred from the state's town tuitioning program, which pays for rural students in districts without public schools to attend schools elsewhere, because they chose a Catholic school. The family argues a 2025 state law sidesteps Supreme Court precedent. — [WORLD](https://wng.org/roundups/lawsuit-says-vermont-is-wrongly-excluding-religious-schools-from-public-benefits-1793724802)

**India tightens its grip on foreign-funded ministries.** India's March amendment to its foreign-contribution law is squeezing Christian charities. The program director of one of the country's largest children's charities told WORLD, "We are not expecting to continue to operate in India beyond this December." A 2016 amendment forced Compassion International out of India after 58 years. Churches that support work in India should ask their partners how they are affected. — [WORLD](https://wng.org/roundups/india-continues-tightening-restrictions-on-foreign-funded-nonprofits-1793980794)

## 🔥 Also Worth Noting

*Notable controversy and media shake-ups — reported with discernment, not appetite.*

**Former Acts 29 chief executive charged with rape.** South Yorkshire Police announced Oct. 29 that Steve Timmis, who led Acts 29 until his 2020 resignation after reports of spiritual abuse, and his wife, Janet, have been charged with eight counts of rape involving one alleged victim, for offenses said to have occurred between May and September 2008. These are charges, not convictions, and the couple had not responded to requests for comment. Police have asked former church members who may have information to come forward. The church Timmis founded, The Crowded House, now Redeemer Church Sharrow Vale, said it was "shocked and saddened"; it commissioned an independent inquiry after 2020 and appointed safeguarding leaders. Pray for anyone harmed. The case is also a sober reminder that a culture of control in a church can hide far worse, and that elders must listen when former members speak up. — [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2026/11/former-acts-29-ceo-charged-rape-uk-spiritual-abuse/)

## 🧭 For the Elder's Desk

*A pastoral takeaway for your leadership team this week.*

> Some of your members will come to church this morning discouraged by Tuesday's results, and some quietly relieved. Neither group needs the pulpit to become a campaign rally. Both need the steady word that Christ reigns, that every child in the womb bears God's image, and that the church's long work of changing hearts goes on. Idaho's result came where pastors had taught and organized for years. The Lifeway numbers point to the same root: a flock that cannot place Saul before David, or recall what the Lord requires in Micah 6:8, will be poorly equipped to weigh ballot language or a magazine's claims about pornography. And the Timmis charges remind us that shepherding includes guarding the sheep from those who would harm them. So ask: **what is our plan, from the pulpit to the children's wing, to help our people actually know the whole storyline of Scripture?** And then: **if a former member came to us with a concern about how leaders treated them, do we have a clear, humble way to listen?**
