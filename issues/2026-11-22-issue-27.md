# State of the Church Today

*A weekly digest of Southern Baptist, Reformed, and broader theological news — curated for pastors and elders.*

**Issue 27 · Sunday, November 22, 2026**

> North Carolina Baptists voted to fold the Biblical Recorder into the state convention, and Texas Baptists approved a project to write their own confession of faith. Both conventions drew crowds, and North Carolina launched a statewide evangelism push toward 2033. The U.S. Catholic bishops met in Baltimore after the midterms and said they will work with whoever holds power. A new Pew report projects that Christians will be a minority in North America by 2070 while the church in sub-Saharan Africa more than doubles. 9Marks writers press the Spirit's work in conversion and the Word's work in creating the church. A week to pray for the nations and for honest, independent church journalism.

## ⛪ SBC & Convention Watch

*Annual meeting, entities, ERLC, resolutions, denominational politics.*

**N.C. Baptists bring the Biblical Recorder inside the convention.** At the 196th annual meeting in Greensboro, Nov. 16–17, messengers approved moving the Biblical Recorder from a separate legal entity to an integrated ministry of the Baptist State Convention of North Carolina. The board cited falling subscription and advertising revenue and rising print costs. The paper keeps its name, its editorial freedom, and its historic purpose; a new board subcommittee will help guard that freedom, and an editorial policy affirming journalistic independence is to come back for board approval. Managing editor Chad Austin wrote to readers, "We are still Biblical. We are still a Recorder." Elders who rely on state papers should watch how independent coverage holds up when the paper reports to the body it covers. — [Biblical Recorder](https://www.brnow.org/news/messengers-approve-integrating-biblical-recorder-into-state-convention/) · [Biblical Recorder](https://www.brnow.org/opinions/editorial/we-are-still-biblical-we-are-still-a-recorder/)

**North Carolina launches ReachNC.** The same meeting drew 2,194 attendees, including 1,652 messengers, the highest attendance since 2008. Messengers elected Steven Wade, pastor of Faith Baptist Church in Youngsville, as president and approved a 2027 Cooperative Program budget of $29,139,500. Executive Director Todd Unzicker launched ReachNC, a statewide evangelism emphasis aimed at more North Carolinians saved and baptized by 2033 than ever before. — [Biblical Recorder](https://www.brnow.org/news/nc-baptists-launch-reachnc-urged-to-own-great-commission-at-2026-annual-meeting/)

**Texas Baptists will draft their own confession.** Messengers to the Baptist General Convention of Texas annual meeting in Waco approved, on Nov. 17, a motion to form an 11-member special committee to propose a Texas Baptist Confession of Faith for the 2027 meeting, funded with $64,000 in non-Cooperative Program money. The sponsor said the goal is a confession written by Texas Baptists that leaves room for congregational liberty. The vote passed over notable opposition, and a Baptist Standard editorial said real concerns about timing, representation, and divisiveness were raised. Context for SBC watchers: Texas messengers rejected a motion in 2024 to affirm the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, and NAMB has said it will not fund church starts with the BGCT unless the statement of faith changes. (Sources differ on the sponsor's first name, Brent or Brian Gentzel.) — [Baptist Standard](https://baptiststandard.com/news/texas/texas-baptist-confession-of-faith-project-approved/) · [Baptist News Global](https://baptistnews.com/article/texas-baptists-will-draft-their-own-confession-of-faith/)

## 🕊️ Theology & Doctrine

*Major debates, books, controversies, council and committee statements.*

**9Marks: you can't make conversion happen.** In an article from *Church Matters* posted Nov. 16, Southern Seminary theologian Gregg Allison explains the Holy Spirit's work in conversion. Repentance, faith, and confession of Christ as Lord are genuine human responses, he argues, but not merely human ones: because we are dead in sin, the Spirit must prompt and empower each of them. A good corrective for churches tempted to treat a decision card or a good program as the cause of new birth. — [9Marks](https://www.9marks.org/article/you-cant-make-it-happen-the-spirit-and-the-doctrine-of-conversion/)

**9Marks: the Word creates the people.** Calvin Anthony, a member of Sovereign Shores Baptist Church in Virginia Beach, offers a biblical theology on Nov. 18: from Genesis to Revelation, God creates a people by speaking, and the church should make the Word central when it gathers, through preaching and the ordinances. Pair it with Allison for a short elders' discussion on what actually builds a church. — [9Marks](https://www.9marks.org/article/the-word-creates-the-people-a-biblical-theology-of-gods-word-for-the-life-of-the-church/)

## 🏛️ Culture & Church-State

*Religious liberty, ethics, politics affecting the church, cultural engagement.*

**Catholic bishops meet after the midterms.** The U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops opened its fall assembly in Baltimore this week. President Archbishop Paul Coakley congratulated those elected and said the conference will work with whoever holds the White House or a congressional majority, while urging the church not to see itself through political or ideological lenses. The AP voter poll cited by the Boston Globe shows Catholics split roughly evenly in House voting, with a divide between white and Latino Catholics. Immigration, abortion, and artificial intelligence are the issues to watch under divided government. — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/11/17/nation/us-catholic-bishops-meet-after-democrats-big-wins-help-reshape-immigration-abortion-debates/)

**Pew: a Western decline, an African ascent.** Pew's new report, *The Future of World Religions*, projects that Christians will make up 46% of North America and 52% of Europe by 2070. Sub-Saharan Africa's Christian population is projected to more than double to 1.6 billion, 54% of all Christians, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo is expected to pass the U.S. as the country with the most Christians. Projections are not prophecy, but they should shape how a church thinks about missions, partnership, and who will send missionaries to whom. — [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2026/11/pew-research-christians-minority-2070-africa-growth/)

**Federal judge: Maine school board violated a church's rights.** WORLD reports that a federal judge ruled the board refused a church's request for a long-term space rental in violation of the Constitution and state accommodations law, though he declined to block the board from denying shorter leases. WORLD's full text is gated, so this rests on its visible summary. Churches renting school space in particular should watch for an appeal. — [WORLD](https://wng.org/sift/maine-school-board-violated-churchs-first-amendment-rights-judge-rules-1794858587)

## 🧭 For the Elder's Desk

*A pastoral takeaway for your leadership team this week.*

> Two Baptist conventions voted this week on how they will govern what they believe and what they report. Texas will write a confession; North Carolina will bring its newspaper under the convention's roof. Neither is a scandal, but both are good prompts for a local church to ask the same questions of itself. Pew's numbers say the church in the West is shrinking while the church elsewhere grows, and 9Marks reminds us where growth comes from: the Spirit gives new birth, and the Word creates the people. So ask: **does our church state clearly what it believes, in writing, and do our members know it?** And then: **with the Week of Prayer for International Missions starting Nov. 29, how will we lead our people to pray for the nations by name?**
